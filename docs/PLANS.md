## Weapon holds: ready stances (2026-11-15)

- **Designer (2026-11-15), on the first fix** (a lean per weapon kind, the sprite turned in the unchanged fist): "worse post fixes. terrible hand holdings ... do research of poses how it looks, looks for references". Complaint shots `art/tests/sheets/hold_complaint_1.png`, `_2.png`.
- **Why both old looks fail:** the base hero has one arm pose, a fist raised at shoulder height, drawn for an upright grip. Upright, every weapon is carried like a torch. Turned 35-40 degrees in that fist, the shaft crosses the knuckles at an angle no wrist allows, and the forearm does not line up with it. A hold is the arm, wrist and fist, not the sprite's angle.
- **Research** (historical fencing guards, poleaxe and axe carrying guides, knife grips, quarterstaff notes, animation posing guides; cartoon and fighting-game ready stances looked at in image search, nothing downloaded or copied). Rules that came out of it:
  - The weapon is posed first and the body adapts; the weapon stays clear of the body's silhouette; long weapons sit on a diagonal, short ones need spread arms and opposing angles.
  - The ready stance is where the blow starts: low or middle guards wait, high guards are wind-ups (those are the `<kind>_wind` poses already).
  - A closed fist holds a handle square to the forearm (sword, axe, hammer, dagger, staff); only a wand, held in the fingers, continues the forearm. The wrist is never bent sideways to tilt a weapon.
- **Stance per weapon kind** (angle: 0 = up, 90 = forward):
  - **Sword:** one-hand low guard. Fist in front of the front hip, arm slightly bent, blade about 50 up and forward at the enemy's chest, free hand back for balance. Why: the common waiting guard (hilt low, point threatening); the blade is a clear diagonal away from the body, and the cut is launched by lifting it to the shoulder, which is the wind-up pose.
  - **Axe:** the sword's low guard. Why: both axes in the game are drawn with one-hand hilts (Stormcleaver has a crossguard, no room for a second fist), and a one-hand axe is carried like a sword, head forward and up. The long axe held at the end of an upright or leaning fist is what the complaint shot shows.
  - **Hammer:** two hands on the haft, a diagonal across the front. Rear fist at the hip near the butt, lead fist forward and higher near the middle, head up and forward. Why: a heavy head needs a second hand near the balance point; the weapon lies on the line through both fists, so it is plainly gripped. The wind-up already has both fists together. Built: the rear fist holds the weapon (the whole shaft drawn in front of the body, `Hand.front`), the lead fist only closes over the shaft (`Hand.cover`).
  - **Dagger** (and the rogue's pair): a knife fighter's guard, both fists clear of the body. Lead fist forward at waist height, blade up and forward; the other fist beside the near hip in a reverse grip, blade down and back, drawn in front of the leg. Why: knives are held close and low; two short blades only read with spread arms and opposing angles. This is nearly the `dagger_wind` pose, so the stab flows from it. The first try had the reverse-grip hand behind the body; the designer saw a blade sticking out of his back, so it was redrawn with that arm on the viewer's side.
  - **Staff:** planted. Butt on the ground by the front foot, near upright, forearm level with the fist at waist height (staffs are gripped at 45% from the top, so the fist has to sit there for the butt to reach the ground), head above the hero's head. Why: every wizard reference carries a tall staff as a third leg; held in a raised fist it floats.
  - **Rods go by length, not by name** (`LONG_ROD` = 0.7 of the hero's height in `src/data/classes.ts`, `weaponKind()` / `Db.WeaponKind`): a rod that long or longer stands planted and swings as a staff, a shorter one is held and swung as a wand. Why (designer, 2026-11-15): a long, top-heavy rod held at its end at 45 degrees looks wrong. Now 11 rods are planted (9 staffs, the Stormcoil Scepter, the Tidecaller Rod) and 2 are wands (Ember Wand, Sparkglass Wand, both 0.45). A planted rod must reach the ground: `(1 - grip) * size` within `PLANTED_REACH` (0.53-0.57), checked by `validateData`, so a new staff with a bad grip fails the export.
  - **Wand:** at the ready, not yet aimed. Elbow bent and tucked in, fist in front of the chest, the wand continuing the forearm up and forward. Why: a wand is pointed, never held upright in a clenched fist. The first try had the arm stretched out at the target; it looked like the cast itself and left the strike pose nothing to add, so it was redrawn bent (the stretched arm is the `wand_strike` pose).
- **Approach compared:**
  - (c) one base, only angles and the fingers sprite: rejected. It is what the first fix did; the arm cannot be right for six different grips.
  - (a) one drawn stance per kind: five of six are needed anyway.
  - (b) shared stances: used where the weapon art makes two kinds the same grip (sword and axe).
  - **Chosen: five drawn ready stances** (`sword_ready` also for axes, `hammer_ready`, `dagger_ready`, `staff_ready`, `wand_ready`), made like the key poses (Qwen edits of `char_hero_bare`, same canvas, scale and feet, legs and torso kept as in the base, own hand anchors). The standing hero shows its weapon kind's ready stance when that picture exists for its outfit, else the old picture with the upright fist. So a blow goes ready, wind-up, step, strike and back to ready through the same pose swaps as now, and outfits without ready art keep working.
  - The lean per weapon kind (`WEAPON_HOLD`, `holdAngle`, `HoldLeans`, `Unlean`) has no job left and is removed.
- **Built on the bare hero (2026-11-15), my own rating against the old upright grip** (sheet `art/tests/sheets/weapon_stances_before_after.webp`; all five beat it, so none was taken out of `READY_POSE`):
  - Sword and axe, low guard: 8/10. Hammer, two hands: 8/10. Staff, planted: 8/10. Short wand, bent elbow: 8/10.
  - Rogue's daggers, second version: 8/10 (the first, with the back hand hidden, was 4/10 and I rated it too kindly). Both fists and both grips are in plain view.
  - Tidecaller Rod: 8/10 planted, pearl at head height (4/10 before, held at its end like a wand). Its grip moved from 0.78 to 0.31.
  - Stormcoil Scepter: 8/10 planted, foot on the ground (6/10 before, foot at shin height). It grew from 0.85 to 0.96 of the hero's height (grip 0.55 to 0.43) so the orb sits above the head; the only size change.
  - The five tall staffs (1.05, 1.08) poked below the boots; their grips went from 0.45 to 0.48-0.49.
  - Swings, one recorded per weapon (sword, axe, hammer, daggers, staff, wand, long rod, scepter): each starts in its stance and comes back to the same stance, no jump in place and no flash of another pose.
  - Weak points left: the pose swap into the wind-up is a hard cut, like every key pose; the stance art has a slightly thicker pale rim than the base picture.
- **The outfits are made (designer: "Run the outfits") and merged (designer, after seeing the sheet: "Yes, commit and push").** All 11 armor outfits have the five stances (`char_hero_<armor>_<pose>`, details in IMPROVEMENTS "Weapon hold"); sheet: `art/tests/sheets/weapon_stances_outfits.webp`. ## Steam launch (research, 2026-10-26)

**Decided (designer, 2026-10-26): first make a game the designer enjoys; Steam work (store page, demo, marketing) starts only after that.** The research is used as design input for now; the launch points below wait.

Market research on 25 indie 2D turn-based or loot-and-class RPGs released on Steam 2025-10 to 2026-10, trip systems, fishing, AI disclosure and market numbers: `docs/research/2026-10_steam_indie_rpg_market.md` (data: `docs/research/2026-10_steam_indie_rpg_comparables.csv`). Sales are third-party estimates; review themes come from ~10 top reviews per game.

- **Visibility decides launches, not features.** 8 of the 9 games with 100+ reviews had a demo, and the hits had thousands of followers or wishlists. Ironwake has almost our feature list (solo developer, 3 classes, 8 elements, affix gear, a town with quests, AI sprites, $7.99 Early Access, no demo): 0 reviews, peak 2 players. Flops often score well (7 of 15 at 85%+) but nobody found them. Half of all 2025 releases got under 10 reviews.
- **Length and price.** Chapters 1-3 are ~2.5 h (pace bot). Games of 2-5 h price at $5-15, median ~$6.50-7; a ~3 h game at $12.99-14.99 (Lastlore Saga) drew "too short" and slid to Mixed; miniquest at $4.99 is 100% positive. The hits run longer (avg playtime 5.6-58 h).
- **Early Access did worse in this set** (median 3 reviews vs 41 for full releases), but those EA games were tiny launches; the one EA hit had a demo and daily patches.
- **AI art:** about half the reach of comparable games (-53% first-month reviews; reaching 100 reviews at ~55% the rate), ratings 3-8 points lower. Worst case is reviewers deciding the disclosure understated the AI use (Dragonwake, Backpack Dungeon). Store and capsule art named as AI drew the most thumbs down. Successful disclosures are longer and specific (median 22 words). Valve's January 2026 rules: AI content players see needs disclosure; code tools don't.
- **What reviews complain about in comparables:** shallow or samey builds, grind, weak itemization (a flood of trash loot, stats not shown on the loot screen), bugs, missing QoL (key rebinding, loot-all, borderless window and aspect ratios). Build variety (next big step) aims at the top complaint.
- **Closest designs in the set:** HellSlave II (single hero, Warrior or Sorcerer into six classes, random loot; solo developer with a publisher, $19.99, 386 reviews, ~10k copies est.): praised for its layered skill system and gear sockets, faulted for grind, damage-sponge trash fights, boring items and a small shared inventory. Duelist: Idle Expedition (7 classes, 21 subclasses, idle trips; 608 reviews, ~33k copies est.): praised for subclass unlocks and auto-equip across a big roster, faulted for a flood of vendor-trash loot and "not truly idle" (menus needing attention). Dungeon Antiqua 2 (FF5-style jobs with cross-job skills, 95%): "some skills strictly better", "too few jobs".
- **Build warnings for the skill plan:** Feywood Wanderers' scaling "pushes one weapon and one element", Lootbound's builds "reduce to stacking one item type": the element match bonus must not make one element best everywhere (our resist rule, +own element/-opposite, already pushes back). Found skills must not be strictly better than others.
- **Fights:** Tap Dungeon is praised for telegraphed enemy intents (we have them) and faulted for RNG one-shot deaths, like Feywood. Effulgence players complained they *couldn't* grind: farming is wanted in this genre.
- **First minutes decide refunds:** low-end flops show refunds at 0.1-0.3 h (Dungeon Forge, Ever Below); Chains on Sand's top complaint is a confusing first fight and thin tooltips. The road and the gate guard are the store's real demo.
- **Trips and alts:** idle players hate babysitting loot (Duelist, Epic Idle Quest II), so trip loot should sort itself (auto-sell or salvage junk) and many characters need auto-equip. Don't tag the game Idler or Auto Battler: those audiences are among the least accepting of AI art.
- **Publisher:** AI-disclosed games with a third-party publisher reached ~3,000 sales 16% of the time vs 7% self-published (correlation, not cause). Press helps: Effulgence's PC Gamer coverage made it the best of the flops.
- **Customization:** Isekai Adventurer Guild is faulted for "little customization": a point for the character creator.
- **Wishlists:** plan on 0.10-0.15 first-week sales per launch wishlist (10,000 wishlists ~ 1,000-1,500 copies in week one).
- **Proposed (from the research):** start a marketing track well before launch: a "Coming soon" page early, a demo (the road and Chapter 1?), Steam Next Fest; launch as a full release, not Early Access; a hand-made (not AI) store capsule; a specific disclosure text (AI art; UI, code and design by hand); key rebinding and display options before launch.
- **Open:** launch at ~3 h for ~$5-8, or grow the game first? When to put the store page up?
